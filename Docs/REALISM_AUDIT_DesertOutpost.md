## How it measures

An object is real only if its **shape**, its **surface** and its **response to light** all pass. Each is measured, never judged from a screenshot.

* **Objects**: every imported model (by prefab), every generated surface (the builder's group and material: the town is merged into chunk meshes, and a fix is made per material in the generator), Unity primitives, each terrain layer, the sky, and each gun's first-person model.
* **Screen importance**: a fixed walk of 602 m (363 views, every 5 m looking ahead and 70 degrees to each side, plus 56 views across the rooms of the houses nearest it), each drawn once with every object in its own id colour and its pixels counted. `screen %` is the object's mean share of the screen over all views; `near` means the walk passes within 8 m of it or it fills a tenth of a view.
* **Shape**: triangles; triangles per m2; bevelled share of the hard-edge length (a 10-60 degree edge beside a strip under 6 cm wide; 60 degrees and over is a hard edge); the share of the surface in plain boxes (8 corners, 12 triangles); wall thickness by casts from behind the face (a shell meets nothing within 1 m).
* **Surface**: maps present, texture size, texels per metre (UV area against world area), how often one texture repeats across a face (95th percentile of a triangle's UV span), albedo variation (std/mean of luminance over the texture's full-size texels), the normal map's mean tilt with its strength applied.
* **Light**: lit shader, shadows received, light probes used by moving renderers, albedo against URP's own validation range (0.01-0.90) and against the real material's range, smoothness against the real material's, metallic only on bare metal, an occlusion map where it is smooth, emission only on lamps. For the objects with evidence, the five-light stage test is measured too (below).
