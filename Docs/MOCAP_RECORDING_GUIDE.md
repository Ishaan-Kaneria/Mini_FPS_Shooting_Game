# How to record your moves

You act a move in front of your phone. The capture pipeline turns the video into an animation for the
player or the enemies. A good video gives a good animation; this page is about getting a good video.

## 1. Phone settings

- **60 frames a second, 1080p.** On most phones: Camera > Video > tap the resolution/frame rate until it says
  1080p 60. Not 4K (slow to process, no better), not 30 fps (fast moves blur).
- **Landscape** (phone on its side).
- **The normal 1x lens.** No zoom, no wide-angle (0.5x), no "cinematic", "portrait video", "action mode" or slow motion.
- **Stabilisation off** if the phone lets you (the phone is on a stand, it needs none, and stabilisation crops
  and shifts the picture).
- **Lock focus and brightness** before you start: press and hold on where you will stand until it says AE/AF
  lock (or similar). Then the picture does not pump as you move.

## 2. Where to put the phone

- **On a stand, a tripod, or propped on a shelf or chair. Never held in your hand**, and never moved during a take.
- **Height:** about waist height, 1 to 1.2 m off the floor.
- **Level:** pointing straight, not tilted up or down. If your phone shows a level line, use it.
- **Distance:** 3 to 4 m for moves done on the spot (punches, kicks, reloads), 4 to 5 m for falls, walking,
  limping and crawling. **Your whole body must stay in the picture the whole time**, from your feet to your hands
  above your head, with some space left around you. Feet cut off is the most common problem: check before you start.
- **Angle**, by kind of move (see the list in section 7 for each move):
  - **Side view (from your right):** stand side-on to the phone, your right side towards it, then turn just a
    little (about 15 degrees, a sixth of the way) towards it. **Punch and kick straight across the picture, never
    towards the phone**: one phone judges distance worst along its own line of sight, so a blow thrown at the
    phone comes out short and bent. Use this for punches, the double punch, the kick and the rifle butt. Your
    punching (right) hand is the one nearer the phone.
  - **Front three-quarter view:** stand facing about halfway between the phone and the side. Use this for the
    reload, hit reactions and deaths.
  - **Side view, moving across:** for falls, getting up, stumbling, limping, walking and crawling, move *across*
    the picture (left to right or right to left), never towards or away from the phone.

## 3. Light, background, clothes

- **Light:** bright and even, from in front of you or from the side. The light behind the phone, not behind you:
  no window or lamp behind you. Daylight in a room with all the lights on is good. Avoid hard, deep shadows.
- **Background:** a plain wall, nothing busy. **Nobody else in the picture**, no mirrors, no TV.
  A floor that is a different colour from your shoes helps.
- **Clothes:** fitted clothes that are a different colour from the wall (for example a dark top, lighter
  trousers). Not baggy: no loose hoodie, long coat or wide trousers, because the tracker has to see where your
  elbows and knees are. Short sleeves or sleeves pushed up are best. Shoes on. Hair tied back, no cap: the tracker
  uses your face and ears for where your head points.

## 4. The rifle stand-in

For moves with the rifle (rifle butt, reload, aiming, anything an enemy does holding his gun), use a **broom or
mop handle, or a stick about 90 cm long**.

- Mark two spots with tape: one **30 cm from the back end** (your right hand, where the pistol grip is) and one
  **60 cm from the back end** (your left hand, under the barrel).
- Hold it the way you would hold the rifle: right hand on the first mark, left hand on the second, the back end
  against your right shoulder when you "aim".
- The pipeline keeps the game's hands on the real rifle's grip and handguard, so it only needs your hands to be
  roughly in those places.
- For moves without the rifle, put it down out of the picture.

## 5. Every take

1. **Start in the neutral pose:** stand straight, feet hip-width apart, arms relaxed at your sides (for a rifle
   move: holding the stick low across your body, as a soldier carries a rifle). **Hold still for 1 second.**
2. **Do the move once,** at full speed and with full effort, the way it would look in a real fight.
3. **Come back to the same neutral pose and hold still for 1 second.**
4. Stop recording. **Do 2 or 3 takes of each move.** I will use the best one.

The still second at each end matters: it is how the pipeline finds "up", the ground and the start of the move.

## 6. The calibration clip (do this first, once per session)

This measures you: how long your arms and legs are, so the animation is scaled right (your height, 175 cm, is
already set). Record it with the phone where it will be for the punches.

1. Stand facing the phone, in the neutral pose, still, for **2 seconds**.
2. Raise both **arms straight out to the sides** at shoulder height, palms down, and hold for **3 seconds**.
3. Lower your arms.
4. **Turn slowly on the spot, one full turn**, taking about 6 to 8 seconds.
5. Stand still facing the phone for 1 second.

Name it `calibration_01.mp4`. Record a new calibration (`calibration_02.mp4`, …) if you change clothes or shoes,
or on another day.

Do them in this order. After each one I process it and show you a preview; nothing goes into the game until

**Enemies:**

## 8. Where the videos go

- Put them in **`~/MocapVideos/`** (the folder `MocapVideos` in your home folder). They stay there; they are
  never copied into the project or into git.
- Names: **who_move_take.mp4**, all lower case: `player_punch_01.mp4`, `enemy_hit_chest_02.mp4`.
  `.mov` files are fine too.
- Then tell me which ones are there.

## 9. If a take came out badly

The preview shows your video next to the animation. Common causes and fixes:

| What you see | Usually | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Feet slide or float | feet left the picture, or the phone moved | Step back, check the feet are in, phone on a stand |
| An arm flips or jumps | it was hidden behind the body | Turn a little more towards the phone |
| Shaky everywhere | dim light, or 30 fps | More light, 60 fps |
| The move is cut short | no still second at the start or end | Hold the neutral pose 1 second each end |
